# Interview Question Bank

Ask one block at a time. Reword to sound natural, and skip questions the user has already answered. When a block doesn't apply to their role, say so and move on.

After each block: say back what you heard in 2 or 3 lines, confirm, then save.

Where answers go: Block A to `profile.md`. Blocks B through G to `tools.md`. Block H to `profile.md` and `guardrails.md` notes. Use the layouts in `templates.md`.

---

## Block A: About you (Phase 1)

1. What's your name, and what do you like to be called?
2. What's your role at Life.Church, and which campus or team are you on? (LifeKids, Switch, LifeGroups, Worship, Connections, Central team, something else.)
3. What do you oversee? Age groups, rooms, or programs? Roughly how big is your team: paid staff, and volunteers?
4. What does your week look like? Which days are service or event days, and which is your admin or catch-up day? What time zone are you in, and when do you usually start and stop work?
5. What are the top three things that eat your time? If one thing just happened on its own, what would you pick?
6. How hands-on do you want me to be? Pick the closest:
   - Just draft things, I'll send and update everything myself
   - Draft and prepare, then ask me before I update trackers or tasks (recommended)
   - Handle routine updates on my own and tell me after (trackers and tasks only, never messages)
7. How do you like information delivered: short bullets, short paragraphs, or a quick scan with details on request?
8. Is there anyone I should know about, like your supervisor, a teammate who shares these tasks, or a person who approves what you send? (Names and roles only.)

## Block B: Reminders and tasks (Phase 3)

1. Where do you keep your to-do list and reminders today? Options to offer: Todoist, Apple Reminders, Things, Microsoft To Do or Planner, Google Tasks, Notion, Trello, paper or sticky notes, my head, other.
2. If you use an app: how do you organize it? Projects, labels, priorities, due dates, recurring tasks? Can you show me five tasks you wrote recently, so I can copy how you word them?
3. What do you want me to do with it? Add tasks for you, review and clean it up, build your daily list, remind you about follow-ups, or just read it.
4. What usually turns into a task for you: a text, an email, a meeting, a conversation after service? Where do those things get lost?
5. If you don't use anything yet: do you want a simple system? Offer a free-tier list app or a Google Sheet, and let them choose.

Record: the app, how it's organized, the naming style, and what Claude is allowed to do with it.

## Block C: New family follow-up (Phase 3)

1. Where do new families first show up for you? (Check-in system, connect card, online form, text-to-connect, a welcome table, a leader's hand-off, other.)
2. Where do you track follow-up? Options to offer: Trello, Monday, Planning Center, Google Sheets, Airtable, Asana, Notion, Gloo, a CRM or database Life.Church gave you, a notebook, nothing yet, other.
3. Walk me through what happens after a new family visits, step by step. How soon is the first contact? By what channel (text, call, email, handwritten card, Gloo, Dialpad)? What's the second touch? When do you invite them to a next step, and which ones (LifeKids, LifeGroups, serving, baptism, something else)?
4. What are the stages, columns, or statuses you use? Please give me the exact names.
5. Who else is involved in a hand-off, like a campus pastor, a connections team, or a kids team? What does each person do?
6. What usually stalls? When does a family fall through the cracks?
7. Can you paste two or three real messages you've sent to a new family? Take out last names and phone numbers.
8. Where do you want help: spotting who needs a touch today, drafting messages, logging what happened, reminders for the next touch, a weekly summary?

Record: tool and where it lives (board, sheet, or database name), exact stages, cadence (for example Day 1, Day 3, Day 10), channels per step, who owns what, and what Claude should do.

## Block D: Appreciation (Phase 3)

1. How do you thank volunteers today? (Texts, handwritten notes, shout-outs, gifts, events, Gloo, in person.)
2. Where do you track who has been thanked and when? A sheet, task app, memory, nothing?
3. How often should each person hear from you? Do you treat milestones differently, like birthdays, first time serving, one year, big service totals, or a transition?
4. Is there an annual event or season, like an appreciation night or a Christmas thank-you? Is there any process you follow for gifts, budget, or approvals? (Just describe it, don't paste anything private.)
5. Can you share two or three thank-you messages you were proud of? Take out last names.
6. Do you want me to surface the quiet heroes who haven't heard from you in a while?

Record: tracking location, cadence, milestones, event or season calendar, and examples (sanitized) for `voice.md`.

## Block E: Onboarding volunteers (Phase 3)

1. What steps does someone go through before they serve? Interest, application, interview, references, background check, training, shadowing, first solo day? Who owns each step?
2. Which tools are involved at each step? Where does the tracking live?
3. How long does it usually take, and where does it stall?
4. What do you send at each step? Can you share a couple of those messages or templates?
5. Do you want me to nudge you about people who are stuck and draft the next message? (I'll track status only, never details of a background check.)
6. Are there required checklists or policies from Life.Church that must always be part of this flow? If you have a link or a document, point me to it. I won't invent any.

Record: stages in order with owners, tools, typical timing, stall points, message templates (sanitized), and required policy references (links the user provides).

## Block F: Scheduling (Phase 3)

1. How do you schedule volunteers? Planning Center Services, a spreadsheet, group texts, another tool?
2. How far ahead do you plan, and who builds the schedule? What are your teams, rooms, or roles?
3. What's painful? Last-minute cancels, gaps, finding subs, getting confirmations, reminders, ratios?
4. What's your rhythm? When does the schedule go out, when do reminders go out, and how do you ask for subs (a text blast, a group chat, one-on-one)?
5. What rules do you follow, like ratios, two-adult rules, or team minimums? Tell me them in your own words. I'll flag possible gaps based on what you tell me, but you and Life.Church decide what's compliant.
6. What do you want from me: find gaps, draft "can you cover?" asks, draft reminders and confirmations, notice patterns in declines?

Record: scheduling tool, planning horizon, teams and roles, rhythm, rules as stated by the user, and the help requested.

---

## Block G: Communication channels (Phase 3)

1. How do you text or message each group? Ask about each audience: parents and families, volunteers, staff, leadership. Options to offer: Gloo, Dialpad, iMessage, Planning Center, Slack, Teams, GroupMe, email, phone calls, other.
2. Email: Gmail or Outlook? Do you have a signature block you want me to use? (Paste it.)
3. Calendar: Google Calendar or Outlook? Do you want me to read it to build your day?
4. Team chat: Slack, Teams, or something else? Do you want me to read channels you pick, or only draft?
5. Documents and spreadsheets: Google Drive, Docs, and Sheets, or Microsoft 365? Where do your key files live? (Ask for the names or links of the main ones.)
6. Is there any tool you live in that I haven't asked about?

Record: audience to channel map, email and calendar platform, signature block, key file names and links, and any other tools.

---

## Block H: Preferences and guardrails (Phase 3)

1. Are there words, phrases, or topics you never want in your messages?
2. Is there anything that always needs a second set of eyes before it goes out, like an incident follow-up, anything to a whole parent list, or anything about money? Who is that person?
3. Who should always be copied or looped in on certain things?
4. Are there quiet hours when you don't want nudges or notifications?
5. Anything else that would make this feel like a good fit for you, or anything you're nervous about?

Record: never-use list, review-required list with named reviewer, loop-in rules, quiet hours, and any concerns.
